## 3. The cost of doing nothing

All figures below are illustrative demo data; the compounding pattern is the point.

At 06:38 the constraint line goes 12% under plan — 369 against a 420 standard on Cascade Twist Dune 240. Today, nobody is watching the cross-system picture, so nothing happens. The counters record it; the meaning doesn't exist yet. For the hours until shift handover, the plant's one unrecoverable resource — constraint time — drains silently, and the projected finish slips past +5h 10m, still opening.

When the slowdown finally surfaces, it arrives as a rate number, not a consequence. Somebody now spends hours across three or four systems discovering what an agent computes in a minute: dye lot DL-4471 is committed to two orders, and one of them — 1,150 linear yards for a fixed install on 18 Aug, crew booked — no longer fits. The date can't move. The re-plan happens by phone, under pressure, uncosted.

And here the trap opens. Splitting the lot across two dye runs looks free — zero changeover against a $1,840 purge — and the person deciding has no way to see that the plant made exactly this trade in June, and April, and May: three claims in four months, one cause, the latest of them DL-4102 → CLM-2291. If the split happens, the two runs won't shade-match, and the fourth claim in that series starts its journey — a future roll, a future batch, a claim that doesn't have an ID yet. Weeks later it lands, the genealogy has to be reconstructed by archaeology, and a credit around $18,400 is issued to close the argument. And the split would be decided on a line already in trouble: roll R-11204 has come off Backing 2 at ΔE 2.9 against a 2.5 tolerance — drift bred by the under-rate run, not by any split — and batch B-88214 has been on hold since 06:52, blocking orders while the grade argument runs. If instead the plant panics safe, the reflex is Saturday overtime — $6,200 where $1,840 would have held both dates.

Either way, the cause analysis dies in a quality report. No route exists for "split lots produce claims" to become a scheduling rule, so the series has no reason to stop at four. Meanwhile the week closes at 97% attainment, and the wall number says everything is fine: $41,200 of margin quietly at risk, $18,400 of it traceable to one sequencing decision that nothing in the current stack would ever connect back.

Each pain is survivable alone. Left together, they compound in one direction: late discovery buys bad options, bad options repeat old mistakes, old mistakes never become rules.

## 4. With and without agentification

### What a classic dashboard / BI / integration project fixes — Class A (11 rows)

Be honest in the room: a substantial share of this register is plain systems integration, and it is worth doing on its own terms. A BI layer over Databricks plus disciplined capture gets you: the self-building report pack (OVR-2/MAK-8), KPI drill-downs (OVR-8), the adherence metric itself (OVR-3/SCH-3), capture-at-machine reason codes (OVR-5/MAK-3), a maintained changeover matrix in your APS (SCH-1/YRN-3), a planning time fence (SCH-6), waste attribution reporting (YRN-4), a live genealogy view (YRN-6/MAK-6), a digital inspection record (QLT-1), searchable claim records (QLT-2), and rule-based auto-grading of clear passes (QLT-5). The demo itself brands these "the low-hanging quick wins — nobody assembles these." What integration alone does **not** do: stay current without a person refreshing it, act on what it shows, or connect what it shows to the decision being made right now.

### What only the agentic layer fixes — Class B (20 rows)

Three mechanics no dashboard has, because they require an agent that acts, decides or learns:

- **The re-plan.** A deviation becomes a consequence and a corrected schedule in minutes, not a planning cycle: detection and notification (OVR-1/MAK-1), the finish-date and at-risk-order computation (MAK-2), the fast path Rowan → Sawyer (SCH-2), the constraint model reading achieved rates live (SCH-7), continuous lot re-sizing (YRN-1), the unprompted cross-agent check (YRN-5), the contextual work order (MAK-7). Lanes: AUTO and THRESHOLDED.
- **The argument against a bad decision.** Claim history surfaces at the moment of choice — "you did this on DL-4102 in June, it produced CLM-2291" (YRN-2/MAK-5), repeat faults linked across rolls and weeks (QLT-6), the margin bridge that prices a sequence decision (QLT-3), the inbox that ranks by costed impact (OVR-4), the cited answer from SOPs and plant history (OVR-6). Flag honestly: the Option B callback is a design proposal, the least-sourced feature in the build.
- **The pattern becomes a rule.** Wren's slow path turns three claims into a hard constraint in Sawyer's model (SCH-5/QLT-4) — the standard changes, not just the report. And the governance that makes all of it adoptable is itself agentic-only: an executable authority matrix (THR-1), stakes-proportionate escalation (THR-2), an institutional decision record that survives turnover (THR-3), a comparable cross-plant autonomy view (THR-4), machine actions that cite the setting they ran under (THR-5), and per-plant tri-state adoption so cautious plants get value at Ask on day one (THR-6). Plus visible autonomy itself (OVR-7) — the answer to "what did it do while I slept?"

The three lanes, wording preserved exactly: **AUTOMATED** — "The agent just does it. Nobody has to ask, chase or put it together." **THRESHOLDED** — "The agent acts inside limits you set. Outside them it stops and asks." **A PERSON** — "Judgement. The agent prepares it — the call is yours."

### What stays human by design — Class C (2 rows, and four boundaries)

Only two register rows are Class C, and that is honest: the never-dos are boundaries, not pains. The dye-lot-or-the-date call (SCH-4/MAK-4) stays a person's — it trades a customer relationship against cost, and the agent can price the trade but cannot own the promise. The grade on the held batch (QLT-7, with QLT-1's decision half) stays a person's — a warranty and brand judgement, not a measurement. Around them stand the four never-dos, verbatim from the spec: **run the line · write the demand plan · grade the product · overrule a quality hold.** Add the meta-rule from the Thresholds section: no agent moves its own dial — the evidence can be assembled by the engine, but the notch is moved by a person, because the setting is the signature. The agents move the run. The people make the calls.
